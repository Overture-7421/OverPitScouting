**SCOUTING**

This Championship marked the most structured and data-driven scouting effort Overture has ever done at any point in FTC during
this season, however, due to logistical issues we had a lot fewer people than what we would like, thus, only Pit-Scouting was done.
Going into Houston, we knew that alliance selection at this scale is a completely different to our regional events you're not
just picking the best robot in your region, you're navigating a field of over 100 teams where every top alliance
pick matters enormously.

**Pit Scouting**
During the pit scouting, our scouts fanned out across the Goodall Division pits with a structured in-house purpose built scouting 
application covering 32 data points per team, from mechanical capabilities like climbing time and shooter type, to programming 
depth like path libraries, odometry, and vision, all the way to competences like drive team experience, commitment level, 
and alliance willingness. Every response fed directly into our Overture Pit Scouting Analyzer, a custom React dashboard we also 
built which parsed the CSV and instantly surfaced charts, statistics, and a ranked Tier List of every scouted team.

**Live Data During Qualifications**
While qualifications ran, we kept the dashboard open on the GraphQL and REST API tabs, pulling live rankings, OPR breakdowns, 
and match scores directly from FTCScout and the official FIRST FTC Events API. The OPR scatter (Auto OPR vs. Driver Control OPR) 
was particularly useful for identifying which robots were genuinely contributing in autonomous versus which were purely 
driver-control scorers, a critical distinction when thinking about alliance composition.

**Tier List and Alliance Preparation**
By the end of qualifications, the Tier List had auto-scored every scouted team using our weighted rubric — rewarding vision, 
odometry, climb capability, autonomous routines, and alliance willingness and assigned each team a tier from S down. 
Our strategy team then dragged cards between tiers based on what we'd actually observed on the field, added notes like 
"climbs consistently" or "auto often fails," and walked into alliance selection with a clean, ranked picture rather than gut 
instinct.

**What We Learned**
- Having a custom tool that combines pit data with live API data in one dashboard is a massive advantage and something we want 
    to keep refining.
- Scouts need clearer guidance on what to prioritize when time in the pit is short at Worlds, teams are busy and windows are 
    narrow.
- The Tier List drag interface was genuinely useful for real-time team debates; it should remain a core part of our selection 
    process.
- OPR alone is not enough, cross-referencing it with pit scouting notes catches things numbers miss, like unreliable autonomous 
    routines or a really strong drive coach.
